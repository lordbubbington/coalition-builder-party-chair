# Coalition Builder: Party Chair — Additional Roles of the State Government

> **Document Status:** Reference Archive & Set-Aside Registry  
> **Purpose:** Catalogs all state government executive offices that have been **set aside** from the primary Statewide Elected Executive tier.  
> **Scope:** Appointed offices, nonpartisan-only elected offices, offices elected by district rather than statewide at-large, offices chosen by state legislatures, and abolished offices.

---

## 1. Exclusion Criteria & Architectural Rationale

In the primary Statewide Elected Executive tier, candidate offices must satisfy three strict gameplay criteria:
1. **Public Direct Vote:** The office is elected by registered voters, not appointed by a governor, picked by a court, or voted on by legislators.
2. **Partisan Ballot Line:** Candidates run under explicit party affiliations (`people` / Democrat, `heritage` / Republican, or third-party lines), allowing party chairs to recruit, fund, triage, and realign voters.
3. **Statewide At-Large Electorate:** The entire state electorate votes on the seat, avoiding the requirement for custom multi-district regulatory boundaries (e.g. PSC district maps).

Offices that fail one or more of these criteria are catalogued below for future mechanics (such as gubernatorial appointment dilemmas, legislative caucus votes, or regional commission map expansions).

## 3. Nonpartisan-Only Statewide Roles

These offices appear on statewide ballots but are legally designated as nonpartisan. Candidates do not run under Democratic, Republican, or third-party ballot lines.

| State | Office | Selection Method | Notes |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **California** | Superintendent of Public Instruction | Nonpartisan Statewide Public Vote | Runoff top-two format on nonpartisan ballot; ideological factions actively endorse out-of-system. |
| **Montana** | Clerk of the Supreme Court | Nonpartisan Statewide Public Vote | Rare judicial administrative executive office; manages high court docket and records. |
| **Nebraska** | Public Service Commission (5 seats) | Nonpartisan District Vote | Part of Nebraska's nonpartisan constitutional tradition (alongside unicameral legislature). |
| **North Dakota** | Superintendent of Public Instruction | Nonpartisan Statewide Public Vote | Nonpartisan general election ballot; education policy leadership. |
| **Oregon** | Commissioner of Labor and Industries (BOLI) | Nonpartisan Statewide Public Vote | One of only 4 labor commissions nationally, but the only one elected on a nonpartisan ballot. |
| **Washington** | Superintendent of Public Instruction | Nonpartisan Statewide Public Vote | Top-two nonpartisan primary and general election; oversees OSPI. |
| **Wisconsin** | Superintendent of Public Instruction | Nonpartisan Spring Public Vote | High-profile spring election on nonpartisan ballot; heavily targeted by teachers' unions and conservative school reform PACs. |

## 4. Offices With Separate Geographic Districts

These regulatory commissions or executive councils represent sub-state regions or districts rather than the statewide electorate. Modeling them requires distinct geographic district maps separate from congressional and legislative districts.

| State | Body | Seats | Election Structure | Regulatory & Political Scope |
|:---|:---|:---:|:---|:---|
| **Georgia** | Public Service Commission | 5 | District-resident candidates; historically voted statewide at-large | Subject to Voting Rights Act litigation; candidates must live in 5 geographic districts. Regulates Georgia Power, electric, natural gas, telecom. |
| **Louisiana** | Public Service Commission | 5 | Single-member geographic districts | Staggered 6-year terms; elected across 5 major Louisiana geographic territories. Regulates public utilities and motor carriers. |
| **Mississippi** | Public Service Commission | 3 | 3 geographic Supreme Court districts | Central, Northern, and Southern districts. Regulates telecommunications, electric, gas, water, and sewer utilities. |
| **Mississippi** | Transportation Commission | 3 | 3 geographic Supreme Court districts | Northern, Central, and Southern districts. Directly governs the Mississippi Department of Transportation (MDOT) and highway contracts. |
| **Montana** | Public Service Commission | 5 | 5 geographic single-member districts | Staggered 4-year terms; regulates monopoly energy utilities (NorthWestern Energy, Montana-Dakota Utilities). |
| **Nebraska** | Public Service Commission | 5 | 5 geographic single-member districts | Nonpartisan district elections; regulates telecommunications, natural gas, grain storage, pipelines. |
| **New Hampshire** | Executive Council | 5 | 5 geographic councilor districts | **Unique US Executive Body:** 5-member elected council holding constitutional veto power over state contracts >$10k, pardons, and all judicial and cabinet appointments. |

## 5. Offices Elected by State Legislatures

In these states, constitutional officers are selected by a vote of the state legislature (often meeting in joint convention) rather than by the general public.

| State | Office | Selection Method | Mechanics & Political Dynamic |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Maine** | Secretary of State | Joint Convention of Legislature | Elected biennially in January by secret ballot of the combined Senate and House. Majority party in legislature picks its loyalist. |
| **Maine** | State Treasurer | Joint Convention of Legislature | Elected biennially by joint session of the Maine Legislature. Oversees state cash management and bond issuance. |
| **Maine** | Attorney General | Joint Convention of Legislature | Elected biennially by joint convention. The only state where the AG is picked by legislative ballot. |
| **Maryland** | State Treasurer | Joint Ballot of General Assembly | Selected by joint ballot of the Senate and House of Delegates for a 4-year term. Key member of the 3-person Board of Public Works. |
| **New Hampshire** | Secretary of State | Joint Session of General Court | Elected biennially by the 400-member House and 24-member Senate. Famous for statutory defense of the First-in-the-Nation presidential primary. |
| **New Hampshire** | State Treasurer | Joint Session of General Court | Elected biennially by the General Court joint session. |
| **Tennessee** | Secretary of State | Joint Convention of General Assembly | Elected for 4-year terms by the combined Senate and House. |
| **Tennessee** | State Treasurer | Joint Convention of General Assembly | Elected biennially by joint convention of the General Assembly. |
| **Tennessee** | Comptroller of the Treasury | Joint Convention of General Assembly | Elected biennially by joint convention; exercises extensive fiscal oversight and audit powers over local governments. |

## 6. Abolished & Inactive Statewide Offices

Offices eliminated by constitutional amendment or statute whose duties were transferred to other departments.

| State | Office | Year Abolished | Successor Entity | Historical Context |
|:---|:---|:---:|:---|:---|
| **Minnesota** | State Treasurer | 2003 | Department of Finance / Minnesota Management and Budget (MMB) | Abolished by constitutional amendment approved by voters in 1998; duties phased out by 2003. |
| **North Dakota** | State Treasurer | 2020 | State Tax Commissioner & Office of Management and Budget (OMB) | Constitutional Measure 2 abolished the office in November 2020; consolidated fiscal operations. |
| **Texas** | State Treasurer | 1996 | Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts | Constitutional amendment proposed by Treasurer Martha Whitehead abolished the office to reduce redundancy; duties merged into Comptroller. |

## 7. Future Mechanical Integration Hooks

When expanding governing mechanics beyond the core campaign game, these set-aside roles provide immediate narrative and mechanical depth:

1. **Gubernatorial Patronage & Appointments (Appointed Roles):**
   - When the player's party controls the Governorship, the Chair is consulted on Cabinet selections (AG, SoS, Treasurer).
   - Pleasing party factions by appointing their ideological champion awards +5 to +10 Faction Happiness; appointing a moderate or rival faction champion triggers faction grievances.
2. **Legislative Joint Conventions (Maine, Maryland, NH, Tennessee):**
   - Contesting state legislative chambers (`stateLegislatures`) directly determines control of these constitutional offices.
   - Flipping the Maine or New Hampshire legislature flips the Secretary of State, Attorney General, and Treasurer in January without a statewide popular vote.
3. **Districted Regulatory Commissions (Georgia, Louisiana, Mississippi, Montana):**
   - If sub-state regional maps are developed for public utilities, these seats can become modular downballot contests tied to localized regional voter models.
